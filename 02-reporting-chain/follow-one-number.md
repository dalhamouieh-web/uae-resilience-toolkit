# Follow one number home

Inspectors rarely read the Board page first. They pick one number and follow it back to its source. Do it yourself before they do.

## How supervisors test the chain

1. Pick one key risk indicator and ask for its written definition, owner and source system.
2. Ask to see each step from source to page: extracts, spreadsheets, manual adjustments, approvals.
3. Compare the same metric in the Board report, the risk committee pack and any group or regulatory report.
4. Check which data quality controls exist, and who signs off before the Board sees the figures.
5. For numbers from providers, ask how the bank checks them.

| Red flags | Green flags |
| --- | --- |
| Numbers assembled by hand each quarter | Extracts automated from source systems; manual steps documented |
| Two definitions of the same indicator | One written definition per indicator, agreed with the owner |
| Vendor figures copied without checking | Provider data checked against the bank's own records or samples |
| Nobody signs off before the Board | First-line owner signs, second line challenges, both recorded |
| A different taxonomy in IT, risk and audit | One taxonomy used by every function |

## Try it

Take one number from your last Board or risk committee report. Use sheet *5 Lineage trace* to record each step. The worked example, *high-severity vulnerabilities past deadline* at Bank Horizon, has 7 steps, 5 of them manual, 3 with no control, and 4 issues found, including two different definitions of "past deadline". Your longest chain is your first data contract.
