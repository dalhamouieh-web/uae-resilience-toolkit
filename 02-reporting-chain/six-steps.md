# Getting there: six steps

Building the chain behind a Board report takes months, not weeks. These six steps are the order that works.

| Step | What to do | What good looks like | Workbook sheet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Clarify roles | Name an owner for every process, control and number. Start from the process universe, with a process owner and a risk owner for each process | No number on the Board page without a named owner | 3 Who does what |
| 2. Anchor | Align the taxonomy and appetite in the ERM framework, and the ORM framework beneath it, with the IT and cyber strategies, all derived from the business strategy | One taxonomy and one rating scale used by IT, risk, compliance and audit | 1 Anchors, 2 Taxonomy |
| 3. Choose indicators | Pick 8 to 12 indicators linked to the appetite and to Critical Operations, including at least one for external threats | Each indicator has amber and red thresholds approved by the Board | 4 Data contracts |
| 4. Write the data contracts | Definition, source, owner, calculation, checks and thresholds for each indicator, including data from providers | Two teams calculate the same number the same way | 4 Data contracts |
| 5. Build the aggregation | Automate extraction where possible, document manual steps, validate in the second line, test with internal audit | The figure can be traced to its source in minutes | 5 Lineage trace |
| 6. Run and improve | Same layout every quarter; review layout and indicators with the Board once a year | Lessons from incidents and audits change the indicators | 7 Readiness |

## It depends on your bank

Proportionality changes how you build the report, never whether the Board owns the appetite, the Critical Operations and the oversight of third parties. See sheet *6 Operating model*: the hard part for a mostly outsourced bank is not the same as for a bank that runs its own IT.
